For learning. Every tracing is a real, de-identified ECG, and every label is the original cardiologist's, not this tool's. Sources are PTB-XL (Wagner et al. 2020) and the PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2021 set — Chapman-Shaoxing, Ningbo, Georgia, CPSC, INCART and PTB — all CC BY 4.0 via PhysioNet. Label review differs by source, and each case says which it is: PTB-XL records whether a human validated the label and only those are taken, while the others are shown as published. Findings are shown as recorded; this page does not interpret.

    The two panels above never move: they are the reference. The one below follows the loop. Limb leads solid, precordial dashed — the limb angles are definitional, the precordial ones a textbook approximation to a direction that depends on the chest.

    A ring with a dot is an axis coming out of the screen at you; a ring with a cross is one going away. On the opening view you are facing the patient's chest, so V1 and V2 — the leads nearest the front of it — are marked coming toward you.

    A normal QRS points left, inferior and slightly posterior — away from anterior, not along it. That is the same fact as the rS in V1 and the tall R in V6 on the tracing above: the vector runs away from V1, which sits anteriorly and to the right. AHA/ACCF/HRS Part III
