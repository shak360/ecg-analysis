A derived vectorcardiogram, not a recorded one. True VCG needs Frank's orthogonal electrodes; these X, Y and Z leads are estimated from the standard twelve by the Kors regression, so the loop is a good approximation and not the thing itself. It is built from the averaged beat, and it is only meaningful when the twelve leads were recorded at the same instant and the rhythm held steady — where they were not, this page says so instead of drawing. Sources are PTB-XL (Wagner et al. 2020) and the PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2021 set, all CC BY 4.0.

The two panels above never move: they are the reference. The one below follows the loop. Limb leads solid, precordial dashed — the limb angles are definitional, the precordial ones a textbook approximation to a direction that depends on the chest.

A ring with a dot is an axis coming out of the screen at you; a ring with a cross is one going away. On the opening view you are facing the patient's chest, so V1 and V2 — the leads nearest the front of it — are marked coming toward you.

A normal QRS points left, inferior and slightly posterior — away from anterior, not along it. That is the same fact as the rS in V1 and the tall R in V6 on the tracing above: the vector runs away from V1, which sits anteriorly and to the right.
